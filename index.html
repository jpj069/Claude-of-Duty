01 — THE WHOLE POINT

There is no assets folder.

Most games ship gigabytes of authored content. This one ships a single JavaScript bundle. The concrete, the brick, the rusted steel, the soldiers, the weapons, the reverb on a gunshot in a stairwell — none of it exists until your GPU computes it at load time. That is why the first load takes a moment, and why the download is smaller than one photograph from most games.

  • 0image, model or audio files in the repository
  • 19procedural surface materials, generated on the GPU
  • 1runtime dependency — three
  • ~500 KBgzipped, the entire game

03 — ELEVEN SUBSYSTEMS

Written from scratch, including the parts nobody writes from scratch.

01

Renderer

An HDR pipeline built by hand, not assembled from examples. Cascaded shadow maps with texel snapping and contact hardening, a depth and velocity prepass, ground-truth ambient occlusion, temporal anti-aliasing with variance clipping, motion blur, a Karis bloom pyramid, GPU metering and an AgX composite.

02

Materials

A texture forge that runs on the GPU. Nineteen surfaces — concrete, brick, plaster, asphalt, sand, rusted and painted and brushed metal, wood, fabric, glass — with periodic noise so everything tiles, height-derived normals, parallax occlusion and curvature-driven edge wear.

03

Physics

No library. A binned-SAH bounding volume hierarchy, a swept-capsule character controller with a five-plane crease stack, impulse rigid bodies with continuous collision, position-based ragdolls and multi-layer bullet penetration.

04

Audio

Synthesised through the Web Audio API with no sound files at all. Layered weapon fire, convolution reverb, HRTF spatialisation and occlusion.

05

Sky

Atmospheric scattering with a time of day, environment maps generated at runtime, volumetric fog and light shafts.

06

Agents

Skinned soldiers with navmesh pathing, perception, cover behaviour and ragdoll deaths — the meshes and their textures generated like everything else.

04 — WHERE IT CAME FROM

One prompt produced this repository.

“I want you to build a first-person shooter at the level of the most recent Call of Duty games. It should be utterly perfect, visually beautiful, with every single thing done at AAA quality — from textures to physics to anything you could think of.”

Roughly 55,000 lines across eleven subsystems followed, written by a fleet of AI agents working against a shared engine contract, with a harsh visual critic looping on each subsystem until it passed. The full prompt and the harness that reviewed the output are both in the repository.

It is a single-player technical demo, and it is honest about that: no multiplayer, no matchmaking, no server. What there is, is a renderer you can read.
